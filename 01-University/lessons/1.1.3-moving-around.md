# 1.1.3 Moving around: `pwd`, `cd`, `ls`, relative vs absolute paths
**Stage / Module:** 1 / 1.1 · **Status:** done · **Date taught:** 2026-11-16
**Builds on:** 1.1.1 (`pwd`, `cd`, `ls`, absolute vs relative paths) and 1.1.2 (shell, aliases). Only the new parts are covered here.

## What you'll learn
- How to move *up* a folder, jump home, and look inside a folder without going there
- Tab completion and command history, the two biggest typing-savers

## Key terms
- **`..` (dot dot):** "the folder one level up", also called the *parent* folder.
- **`.` (dot):** "the folder I'm in right now".
- **`~` (tilde):** a shortcut for your home folder, `C:\Users\aidan`.
- **Tab completion:** press Tab partway through a name and the shell fills in the rest.
- **Command history:** the shell remembers what you typed. The Up arrow brings it back.

## The problem this solves
Typing full folder names is slow, and one typo means "path not found". Tab completion removes typos, the Up arrow saves retyping, and `..` and `~` let you get anywhere in a few keystrokes.

## Explanation
`..` and `.` are relative-path building blocks (from 1.1.1). `..\02-Knowledge` means "go up one level, then into 02-Knowledge". `~` works like an absolute path to home: `cd ~\AI-Program` works from anywhere.

`ls` can take a path. `ls ..\02-Knowledge` shows that folder's contents *without moving you*.

PowerShell's Tab completion writes names as `.\Name`, which means "Name, inside the current folder". The `.\` is harmless.

## Example from your projects
Later you'll have `~\AI-Program`, `~\trading-research` and `~\content-agent`. Moving between them is `cd ..\trading-research` (they're neighbours) or `cd ~\trading-research` (from anywhere).

## Hands-on exercise
Start in `C:\Users\aidan\AI-Program`.
1. `cd ..` and the prompt shows `C:\Users\aidan`
2. `cd ..` and the prompt shows `C:\Users`
3. `cd ~` and the prompt shows `C:\Users\aidan`
4. Type `cd AI-P` and press **Tab**. It completes to something like `cd .\AI-Program`. Press Enter.
5. Type `cd 01` and press Tab, then Enter. You're in `01-University`. Run `ls` and expect `exercises`, `lessons`, `CURRICULUM.md`, `PROGRESS.md` and `ROADMAP.md`.
6. `ls ..\02-Knowledge` shows `concepts`, `decisions`, `projects` and `sessions`, but your prompt doesn't change.

### Lessons from doing it (2026-11-16)
- `cd` on its own (no `..`) does nothing in PowerShell: no error, no movement.
- Copying from a formatted table can bring a border character like `│` along. The error "is not recognized as the name of a cmdlet" means: look at the first word on the line.
7. Press the **Up arrow** a few times. Your earlier commands reappear. Press Esc to clear the line.
8. `cd ~\AI-Program` takes you back to the program folder from anywhere.
