# Tool review: Google Veo (and its replacement, Gemini Omni Flash), via Google Flow
**Source:** user request (`/tool-review Google Veo`) for the myth-busting channel's visuals, after the code-drawn bulls prototype was rejected · **Reviewed:** 2026-11-20
**Checked against (official pages read 2026-11-20):** support.google.com/flow/answer/16352836 (Flow models and features), support.google.com/flow/answer/16526234 (Flow credits), support.google.com/labs/answer/16353334 (Flow help), one.google.com/about/google-ai-plans (truncated; some figures came from a summary of the page), gemini.google/overview/video-generation, aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing, /deprecations, /video, /omni (the old ai.google.dev pages now redirect there), policies.google.com/terms (effective 2026-07-30), policies.google.com/terms/generative-ai/use-policy, support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491 (YouTube AI disclosure), support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392 (YouTube monetization policies), tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity (effective 2026-09-24), newsroom.tiktok.com C2PA post (2024-05-09).
**Could not read:** TikTok's AI-content help article (the page came back empty). **Not verified:** whether Flow puts a visible watermark on videos for some plans; the exact price of each plan (the Pro figure of $19.99/mo comes from the page's savings calculator); Flow's own service terms.
**Label:** established (big vendor, widely used), but the **model line changes fast**. Veo is being retired in favor of "Gemini Omni Flash".
**Verdict:** implement now, **as a free trial only** (no paid plan yet). Test one bulls video on Flow's free daily credits. Decide on a paid plan only after the trial result.

**Terms used here:**
- **Text-to-video / image-to-video model:** an AI model that turns a written description (a **prompt**), and optionally a picture, into a short video clip.
- **Flow:** Google's website for making AI videos. You type prompts, pick a model and download clips. Nothing to install.
- **Credits:** Flow's in-app currency. Each clip costs some credits, depending on the model, length and resolution.
- **Ingredients / reference images:** pictures you upload (e.g. your paper-cutout bull) so the model keeps that character looking the same from clip to clip.
- **Aspect ratio 9:16:** a tall, vertical frame, the shape TikTok and Shorts use.
- **Resolution (360p / 720p / 1080p):** how many lines of pixels tall the video is. Higher means sharper; 1080p is the usual standard for Shorts.
- **SynthID:** Google's invisible watermark, a hidden signal in the pixels that software can detect. Viewers can't see it.
- **C2PA / Content Credentials:** a standard label stored inside the file's data that says "made with AI". YouTube and TikTok read it and can label your post automatically.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What is it? | Google's family of AI video models. As of today, **Veo 3.1 is being phased out**. The Gemini API shut down Veo 3.1 on 2026-10-22, and in Flow, **Veo 3.1 Fast, Veo 3.1 Quality and Omni Flash 1.1 retire on 2026-12-05**. What remains in Flow is **Omni Flash 1.2** (the new main model) and **Veo 3.1 Lite**. For a creator, the realistic way in is the **Flow** website, either free or through a Google AI plan (Plus, Pro or Ultra). |
| What problem does it solve? | It produces the short, stylized clips (an animated bull, a paper-cutout look) that code-drawn graphics couldn't reach. That is the exact gap left after the prototype was rejected. |
| How does it work? | In Flow you write a prompt (e.g. "paper-cutout style bull, vertical, flat colors, stage-lit arena"), add reference images if you want a consistent character, then pick the model, length and aspect ratio and generate. You download the clips and cut them together in CapCut under the voiceover. |
| How does it plug into Claude Code? | It doesn't need to. The content agent already writes the script, and it could also write a **shot list with one prompt per clip**, which you paste into Flow. A programmatic route exists (the Gemini API with `gemini-omni-1.2-flash`), but it costs more per video and needs a key. It isn't needed now. |
| Advantages | **Vertical 9:16 is supported natively** (the Omni docs show `aspect_ratio: "9:16"`; Flow says "both aspect ratios" for every model). **Reference images help keep a character consistent**: in Flow, Omni Flash 1.2 supports "Ingredients", and Veo 3.1 Lite supports them for 8-second clips only. The API allows up to 6 reference images (3 per character, 2 characters). Clips run 3–10 s free, or up to 15 s on a paid plan, which suits a cut-every-few-seconds Short. There are **50 free credits every day, with or without a subscription**. No install and no key. Google states it won't claim ownership of what you generate. |
| Disadvantages | **The models keep changing.** Veo was largely replaced within weeks, so tutorials and prompts go stale. **Consistency is helped, not guaranteed**: AI clips drift in style and detail between generations, so expect re-rolls (redos). Free generation may be blocked at **peak hours (about 2–5 PM UTC, which is 9 AM–12 PM US Eastern)**. Free and Plus users generate at 360p or 720p; 1080p comes only through upscaling (the cost wasn't stated). It wasn't verified whether a visible watermark appears on free or lower plans. That matters for a clean look, so check it on the first downloaded clip. |
| Cost (rough monthly) | **Per clip in Flow:** Omni Flash 1.2 costs **1 credit per second at 360p** and **3 credits per second at 720p** (a 10 s clip at 720p is 30 credits). Veo 3.1 Lite costs **10 credits per 4/6/8 s clip at 720p** (5 on Ultra). **Per video** (about 70 s of footage, roughly 12–15 clips, plus about 1 redo per clip): roughly **300 credits on Veo 3.1 Lite** or **about 420 credits on Omni Flash 1.2 at 720p**. **Free:** 50 credits a day is up to about 1,500 a month, or roughly **3–5 videos a month for $0** if you generate on most days (credits don't roll over, and peak hours can block you). **Google AI Pro (about $19.99/mo, unverified)** adds 1,000 credits a month on top of the free ones, for roughly 2–3 more videos a month, about **$7–10 per extra video**. Plus adds 200 credits a month (price not captured). **API alternative:** Omni 1.2 Flash at about **$0.07/s at 720p** (from a page summary, unverified) is about **$10 per video** with redos, which is over budget. |
| Difficulty | Low for the tool itself (a website, prompts, download). Medium for getting good results: writing consistent prompts, preparing reference images and cutting in CapCut take practice. |
| Security (data it can reach, third-party code, keys, trifecta legs) | **Reaches:** only what you type or upload (prompts and reference images go to Google). **Third-party code on the PC:** none (it runs in the browser). **Keys:** none for Flow (you sign in with a Google account); the API route would need a key in `.env`. **Trifecta:** adds **no leg**. It isn't connected to the agent, your files or any posting tool. Don't upload private photos (e.g. of yourself for "@me" avatars) unless you mean to. |
| Platform-rules risk (content / e-commerce / trading) | **YouTube:** disclosure is required only for AI content "that seems realistic". Its own examples of non-realistic content needing no disclosure include a "fully animated video". **But** YouTube may auto-label videos that carry C2PA data or that its systems detect as AI, and those labels **can't be removed**. For animated content the label sits in the expanded description, and disclosing "won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money". **The bigger YouTube risk is monetization:** the "inauthentic content" policy bans "mass-produced, generic, repetitive" AI content, "templated storylines", and "videos that stitch together unrelated or inconsistent AI clips". It allows "using AI to visualize a unique character and narrative you invented". Researched, sourced myth-busting with a consistent recurring character fits the allowed side; a churned-out template wouldn't. **TikTok:** you must label "realistic-looking" AI scenes or people. TikTok reads C2PA data and can auto-label posts. Unlabeled content "may be removed, restricted, or labeled". **Program rule:** turn the AI label on **every time** on both platforms, even for cartoons. It costs nothing and removes the risk. **Google's rules** forbid presenting AI content as made solely by a human. |
| Overlap with what I already have (STACK.md) | None. Nothing in the stack makes video. `make_video.py` (code-drawn) was rejected; `make_pack.py` (voiceover, captions, checklist) still complements this. CapCut isn't in STACK.md yet. |
| Helps which project? | **Content agent** (TikTok and YouTube Shorts myth-busting), directly. It could later help e-commerce product videos (a separate review). |

**Reasoning for the verdict:**
This solves a problem the user has **today**: the content agent produces passing scripts, but there is no acceptable way to turn them into video, and nothing in STACK.md covers it. It also needs no install, key or code, and the free daily credits mean the trial costs $0. So the honest call is "implement now", but only as a **free trial on one video** (the bulls draft). A paid plan is premature until we know three things: whether the paper-cutout look stays consistent across about 12 clips, whether free downloads carry a visible watermark, and how many redos a usable clip really takes. Also keep it in proportion: this is a production tool, not part of the foundation. The current lesson (4.4 evals) and the `content-agent` backup still come first. One limit of this review: it covers Google's tool only. Competing AI video tools (e.g. Runway, Kling) were not compared, so "best value" isn't established.

**What would change the verdict:**
- The trial shows a **visible watermark**, unusable consistency, or many redos per clip → review a competitor before paying anything.
- The free credits prove too few for the posting schedule → consider Google AI Pro (about $20/mo) for one month and measure the cost per finished video.
- Google changes the models or credit prices again (likely) → re-check the Flow credits page before paying.
- The channel wants fully automated production → re-review the **API route** (Omni Flash via the Gemini API) at Stage 5. That would add a key, a cost of about $10 per video, and a pipeline to test.
