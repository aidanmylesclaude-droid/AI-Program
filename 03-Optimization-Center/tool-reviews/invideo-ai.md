# Tool review: InVideo AI
**Source:** user request (content agent, after the code-drawn video was rejected) · **Reviewed:** 2026-11-20 · **Checked against:** invideo.io/pricing, invideo.io FAQ "which pricing plan is best for AI video creators" (updated 2026-09-09), invideo.io/terms-and-conditions (sections 3–4 read verbatim; the rest only summarised), help.invideo.io (index page, plus search snippets of the "replace media" and "iStock" articles), TikTok support "How is AI-generated content labeled", YouTube Help "Disclosing use of altered or synthetic content", YouTube channel monetization policies + Spam policy.
**Label:** established (big, long-running commercial product). Its *generative* (made-from-scratch AI video) side is newer and changing month to month.
**Verdict:** implement now, as a **one-month trial on the cheapest monthly plan, stock footage only**, after the visual-format decision and with the conditions below. Cancel if two test videos aren't postable.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What is it? | A website (no install) that turns a script or prompt into a finished video: it picks **stock footage** (real video clips licensed from libraries such as iStock), adds an AI voiceover, captions and music, and lets you edit by typing commands. It can also make **generative clips** (video invented by an AI model) with models like Seedance, Kling and Veo. |
| What problem does it solve? | Today's blocker: the agent writes a good 65–75 s script, but nothing turns it into a video that looks good enough to post. InVideo gives real-looking footage in minutes, so you aren't drawing shapes in code. |
| How does it work? | Paste the script, choose vertical (9:16) and the voice. It builds scenes, and you fix each one by typing in the "Magic Box" (e.g. "Change the 2nd media to a bull in a field"), shuffling or searching the stock library, or uploading your own clip. **Not verified:** whether it keeps a pasted script word for word (no official page says so). Test this first; tell it "Settings: use my script exactly, do not rewrite". |
| How does it plug into Claude Code? | It doesn't, and that's fine. Docs mention a CLI (a command-line tool), but that's **not** for now: it would mean another key and automated posting. Workflow: the agent writes the draft + `make_pack.py` pack → you paste the script into InVideo → export → finish in CapCut desktop → you post by hand. |
| Advantages | Real footage instead of crude shapes; fast; you can swap any scene; you can turn iStock off ("Make iStock media 0") to save premium allowance; you finish in CapCut, which you already know. |
| Disadvantages | Stock picks are often generic or slightly off-topic, so expect to fix 3–6 scenes per video. Generative clips can **invent things that contradict the facts** (e.g. a bull charging a red cape when the truth is about movement, or a goldfish doing something unreal). Many faceless channels use the same stock + AI-voice look, which is a YouTube monetization risk (see Platform rules). Pricing pages contradict each other. Credits expire monthly. |
| Cost (rough monthly) | **Unclear: confirm on the pricing page before paying.** Pricing page (2026-11-20): Starter **$10/mo billed annually** (normally $20), 400 credits, 100 premium stock clips; Plus $30/mo annually, 2,000 credits; Max $62.50–125. The official FAQ instead lists Plus $20, Max $100, Generative $200. The per-video credit cost for a stock-footage Short **isn't stated**. Generative clips cost about **56–96 credits per 5 seconds**, so one 70 s all-generative video could use ~800–1,350 credits: **much more than Starter covers**. No free plan is listed (the terms say free outputs "may carry a visible watermark"). **Advice:** pay one month (not the annual plan), stock only, and count videos per month. A few dollars per video is realistic only on stock footage. |
| Difficulty | Easy: a website plus some editing judgment. The real work is checking every scene against the facts. |
| Security (data it can reach, third-party code, keys, trifecta legs) | Website only: no code on your PC, no key, no connection to Claude Code, so **no trifecta leg added**. It sees only what you paste (scripts) and upload. Terms 4(f): by default outside the EEA/UK InVideo **may train AI models on your uploads**. Turn that off in its privacy settings (your click; opt-out isn't retroactive). Don't upload your face or voice. Use a strong unique password. |
| Platform-rules risk (content / e-commerce / trading) | **AI labels.** TikTok requires the "AI-generated content" toggle for realistic AI-made scenes or people. YouTube requires "AI use: Yes" in Studio for realistic, meaningfully AI-generated or altered content (Shorts get an on-video label). Real stock footage + captions doesn't need a label. **Any generative clip that looks real does: label it.** InVideo may embed C2PA content credentials (a hidden "made with AI" tag that TikTok reads and can auto-label), and you mustn't strip them. Whether a generic AI narrator voice alone needs a label isn't clearly stated: label it if unsure (falsely labeling *real* footage is also against TikTok's rules). **YouTube monetization:** the "inauthentic content" policy (renamed July 2025) and the Spam policy name generic-template AI videos with repetitive imagery and AI voice reading an AI script. Your protection is real research, your own angle, and varied visuals, not volume. **Accuracy:** a misleading visual in a myth-busting video can plant the myth it's busting, so check every scene. Stock is safer than generative for this. Licensing: terms 4(h) allow commercial use, but you're responsible for third-party clearances, and InVideo doesn't promise you own the output. |
| Overlap with what I already have (STACK.md) | Nothing in STACK.md makes videos. **CapCut desktop** (you use it, though it isn't in STACK.md) covers editing, captions and probably a stock library, so InVideo adds the "script → first cut with footage" step. **Worth checking before paying:** whether CapCut's own script-to-video or stock features are good enough (not researched here). |
| Helps which project? | Content agent (TikTok + YouTube Shorts myth-busting). Production only; it doesn't change the agent. |

**Reasoning for the verdict:** This solves a problem you have *today* (no usable video), nothing in STACK.md covers it, it adds no code, keys or trifecta legs, and one month costs about the price of a few API runs. It's a production tool, not an agent tool, so it doesn't pull you off the main path, as long as Stage 4 (4.4 evals) carries on alongside it. Conditions:
1. **First** decide the visual format (e.g. "real stock footage + bold captions + AI voice").
2. Monthly plan only, and **generative clips off by default**.
3. Paste the bulls script and check three things: the words are unchanged, every scene matches the facts, and the result is good enough to post. If any check fails twice, cancel.
4. Label on TikTok/YouTube whenever a realistic generative clip is used.

**What would change the verdict:**
- CapCut's own script-to-video looks good enough → **ignore** InVideo (no new subscription).
- InVideo rewrites the script, or the stock picks keep contradicting the facts → **ignore**.
- You want mostly generative visuals → the credit cost probably blows the budget. Re-review the higher tiers or other tools.
- You want the agent to send scripts to InVideo automatically (CLI/API) → **Stage 5**, with a fresh security review.
